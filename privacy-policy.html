Advertising and Premium

The free version of the app shows ads through Unity LevelPlay, which serves them from Unity Ads. There are three kinds:

  • a banner at the foot of some screens;
  • now and then a full-screen ad when you go back from a weapon to the list (at most one every 10 minutes, and none in your first two days);
  • a short ad you choose to watch before reading a stash photo.

To show and measure these ads, Unity and its ad partners may collect your device’s identifiers (the advertising identifier only if you allow tracking), your IP address and the rough location it gives, your device model, language and connection type, and how you interact with ads. I don’t receive this data.

If you live in the EU, the EEA, the UK or Switzerland (going by your device’s Region setting, or on Android also its SIM or network country), the app first asks whether ad partners may use this data to personalise ads. “Keep ads general” still shows ads, just not personalised ones, and you can change your answer at any time under Customise > Personalised ads.

On iPhone and iPad the app then asks, through Apple’s App Tracking Transparency, whether it may track you. If you don’t allow it, your advertising identifier isn’t shared. You can change that answer at any time in Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.

On Android there’s no such question: your device’s advertising ID is shared with the ad partners unless you’ve deleted it or opted out in your device’s Settings > Privacy > Ads (or Settings > Google > Ads).

Stash photos are read on your device and are never sent to Unity or to me. On Android the text in them is read on your device by Google’s ML Kit, which comes through Google Play services; the photos aren’t uploaded.

Premium (a yearly subscription or a one-off purchase) removes the ads, and once you have it the app no longer starts the ad services. Purchases are handled by Apple on iPhone and iPad and by Google Play on Android; I never see your payment details. Premium’s iCloud sync keeps your progress in your own iCloud account, which I can’t see.

On Android, your progress and settings are included in Android’s own backup to your Google account, so they come back when you set up a new phone. That backup is Google’s, and I can’t see it; you can turn it off in your device’s backup settings.

Log Data

I want to inform you that the app doesn’t collect crash reports or usage statistics for me. If you send a data report from the app, it is an ordinary email from your own Mail app: I see your email address and whatever the email contains, and use them only to answer you and fix the data. The app downloads weapon and camo updates and pictures from jsDelivr, GitHub and this website, which see your IP address as any website does.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, the app on iPhone and iPad tells this website the push token Apple gives it for your device, along with the app’s language and version, so that announcements of new seasons, events and camos can reach you as soon as they’re out. Nothing else about you or your progress is sent. The token is used only to send those announcements, never for advertising or tracking, and it is deleted as soon as Apple reports it no longer works (for example after the app is deleted). If you turn notifications off, no more announcements are sent; to have the token removed straight away, email the address below.

On Android, the app checks for new content itself every few hours and shows the notification from your device; no token or other information is sent for it.

Stream Overlay

The stream overlay is off until you switch it on. While it’s on, the app sends this website the camo figures it shows (the game, mode and weapon you picked and their progress) so the overlay page can display them, along with the channel name if you type one, which only labels how often the overlay’s QR code is scanned. The overlay’s link is a random id that isn’t tied to you. Switching the overlay off deletes the figures from the website.
